The shape of the trip

One rental car, three bases in the mountains, and very little time spent getting from one place to the next. The idea is to see the Japanese Alps at the peak of the autumn colours without spending the holiday in a car or on a train.

6nights in the mountains, away from the cities
3of those nights in Kamikochi, the high valley that is the heart of the trip
6hof driving in total, spread across all nine days
3nights in Tokyo: two at the start to recover from the flight, one at the end before the early train to the airport

Open the full route in Google Maps

The route is a simple loop. You take the train from Tokyo to Matsumoto, pick up a car there, and head south into the Kiso valley, where the old post towns of Tsumago and Magome sit on the Nakasendo, the Edo-period road that linked Tokyo and Kyoto through the mountains. From there you drive back north, stopping at Narai-juku on the way, to Hirayu, where you leave the car and take the shuttle bus into Kamikochi for three nights. On the way out you go up the Shinhotaka ropeway and spend a final mountain night at a hot spring inn in Okuhida, then drive back to Matsumoto, return the car, and take the train to Tokyo.

Only about six hours of driving across the whole nine days, and on five of those days you do not touch the car at all.

DAY 1 · FRIDAY 23 OCTOBER

Land, and do almost nothing

A quiet day in Tokyo. It is there so that she is properly rested before she starts driving the next day.

Morning

Your flight, Scoot TR 882, lands at Haneda Terminal 3 at 01:05, after leaving Singapore at 17:15 the evening before. Take a taxi straight into town, which takes about 35 minutes and costs around 9,000 yen at that hour. You should be in bed by about half past two, and then you can sleep for as long as you like, because nothing is planned for the morning.

Afternoon

A slow wander around the city with nothing booked and no time pressure. One place worth the trip is Dashi Okume at Azabudai Hills, which your friend recommended. It is a shop where you mix your own blend of dashi, the Japanese soup stock, to take home with you.

Evening

Dinner with her friend in Tokyo. After a short night on the plane, it is best to keep it relaxed and not too late.

Flight
Scoot TR 882. Singapore Changi Terminal 1, Thu 22 Oct 17:15, to Tokyo Haneda Terminal 3, Fri 23 Oct 01:05. 6h50.
Driving
None today.
Public transport
A taxi from Haneda to Shinjuku, about 35 minutes. After that, the metro for getting around the city.
Where you sleep
A hotel in Tokyo, near Shinjuku. Tell the hotel in advance that you will arrive at around 3am, so they keep the room for you.

You stay two nights in the same Tokyo hotel, so there is no packing on the 23rd and you can leave your bags in the room all day.

DAY 2 · SATURDAY 24 OCTOBER

Into the Kiso valley

From Tokyo to Tsumago: a scenic train ride into the mountains, then a two-hour drive down a quiet river valley.

Morning

After breakfast, take the Azusa limited express from Shinjuku station to Matsumoto. The ride takes two hours and forty minutes and is beautiful in its own right, climbing out of the Tokyo suburbs and running past vineyards and mountain ranges as you head into Nagano.

Afternoon

Pick up the rental car at Matsumoto station. This is her first drive on the left, and the road south through the Kiso valley is a gentle place to start: it follows the river through forested hills and small towns. The house in Nagiso is just 3 km from Tsumago. You should arrive by mid-afternoon, in daylight and without rushing. Check-in is from 16:00.

Evening

Drive seven minutes into Tsumago and walk around the village once the day-trippers have left. Tsumago is one of the best-preserved post towns on the Nakasendo: its main street of dark wooden inns and shops looks much as it did two hundred years ago, and it is at its most atmospheric in the early evening when it empties out. The house does not serve dinner, so ask the host to book a restaurant for you ahead of time.

Driving
Matsumoto to Nagiso, about 110 km and 2 hours. Her first drive on the left, on an easy valley road.
Public transport
Azusa limited express from Shinjuku to Matsumoto, 2h40. Reserve your seats in advance.
Where you sleep
tsumugi tei, Nagiso (booked). A renovated wooden house, one minute from Nagiso station. You have the whole house to yourselves, with free parking.

DAY 3 · SUNDAY 25 OCTOBER

The Nakasendo walk

An 8 km walk, mostly downhill, along the old highway that connected Kyoto and Tokyo in the days of the samurai.

Morning

Drive to Tsumago and park there. Take the local bus up to Magome, a village built on a steep hillside with a stone-paved main street, then walk the Nakasendo trail back down to Tsumago and the car. The path runs through cedar forest and bamboo, past old stone paving, small shrines and two waterfalls. Your friend did this walk and says it is still just as beautiful. Allow about three hours. Start after nine o'clock so the morning frost has time to melt off the stones.

Afternoon

Spend the rest of the day in Tsumago at your own pace, looking into the old inns and craft shops. Or, if you want more, drive 40 minutes to the Atera gorge for an easy walk along a clear green river, with the autumn leaves at their best.

Evening

A second night at the same house, so there is nothing to pack and nowhere to be. Dinner is at a restaurant booked ahead through the host.

Driving
Nagiso to Tsumago and back, 7 minutes each way. The Atera gorge is optional, 40 minutes each way.
Public transport
Local bus from Tsumago up to Magome, about 25 minutes.
Where you sleep
The same house, tsumugi tei in Nagiso.

There is a luggage service that carries bags between Magome and Tsumago, about 1,000 yen per bag, dropped off by 11:30. You will not need it, because your bags stay at the house, but it is good to know it exists.

DAY 4 · MONDAY 26 OCTOBER

North to Kamikochi

One stop at another old post town on the way, then you leave the car behind and take the shuttle bus up into the valley.

Morning

Drive north up the Kiso valley. Stop at Narai-juku, another post town on the Nakasendo. It has the same long street of old wooden buildings as Tsumago but only about a third of the visitors, so it feels calmer. An hour is enough to walk its length and have a coffee.

Afternoon

Continue through the mountains to Hirayu. Park the car there and take the shuttle bus into Kamikochi, which takes 25 minutes. Private cars are not allowed in the valley at all, and that is a big part of why it has stayed so quiet and unspoilt.

Evening

Walk out to Kappa Bridge, the wooden footbridge over the river in the middle of the valley, just as the last day-trip buses are leaving. From the bridge you look straight up at the Hotaka mountains. Then a soak in the lodge's hot spring bath, followed by dinner at the lodge.

Driving
Nagiso to Narai to Hirayu. About 140 km, around 3 hours including the stop at Narai.
Public transport
Shuttle bus from Hirayu to Kamikochi, 25 minutes. Pack a small bag for three nights, because the big suitcases stay in the car.
Where you sleep
A lodge with its own hot spring, inside Kamikochi.

DAY 5 · TUESDAY 27 OCTOBER

The long one

About 20 km there and back along the Azusa river, almost completely flat the whole way.

Morning

Start at Kappa Bridge and walk to Myojin, where there is a small shrine beside a still pond, then carry on to Tokusawa, an open meadow with old trees. The path stays level beside the river, and the Hotaka range towers above you for the whole walk. In late October the larch trees in the valley turn a bright golden yellow.

Afternoon

Carry on to Yokoo, where the path ends and the serious mountain climbs begin, and then turn back the way you came. It is about six hours of walking in total, with no climbing and nothing difficult, just a long and very satisfying day outdoors.

Evening

A long soak in the hot spring, dinner at the lodge, and an early night.

Driving
None. The car stays parked at Hirayu.
Public transport
None. Everything today starts and ends on foot at the lodge door.
Where you sleep
The same lodge in Kamikochi.

DAY 6 · WEDNESDAY 28 OCTOBER

The climb, or the spare day

If the sky is clear, climb up to Dakesawa. If it is not, keep the day free and enjoy the valley instead.

Morning

Climb up to Dakesawa, a high hollow below the Hotaka peaks. It is 700 metres of climbing and the path is steep, but there are no chains or ladders and no exposed drops. From the top you look down over the whole Kamikochi valley. Allow four to five hours there and back.

Afternoon

Come back down slowly and rest your legs. If the weather is bad, skip the climb altogether and instead repeat whichever walk you liked best in the valley.

Evening

The hot spring again, and your last dinner in the valley.

Driving
None.
Public transport
None.
Where you sleep
The same lodge in Kamikochi.

This is the weather day. In late October you can expect one or two bad days out of every four, and this is the day you can afford to lose.

DAY 7 · THURSDAY 29 OCTOBER

Dawn at the pond, then the hot springs

You leave the valley, ride the ropeway up into the high mountains, and then spend the rest of the day doing nothing at all.

Morning

Get up early and walk to Taisho Pond at first light. The pond was formed in 1915, when an eruption of the Yakedake volcano blocked the river, and dead trees still stand in the water. On a still morning the volcano is reflected in the surface and there is frost on the grass. Afterwards, take the shuttle bus out of the valley back to Hirayu and your car.

Afternoon

Drive a short way to the Shinhotaka ropeway. The cable car, which has a two-storey cabin on its upper section, takes you up to 2,156 metres. From the viewing deck at the top you get the full high-alpine panorama of the Northern Alps without having to walk on ice or snow. Then drive to the inn at Okuhida and check in by about two o'clock.

Evening

Your room has its own private open-air hot spring bath, so you can soak outside whenever you like. Dinner is kaiseki, a traditional meal of many small seasonal courses. There is nothing else to do tonight.

Driving
Hirayu to the Shinhotaka ropeway, then on to your inn. About 30 minutes in total.
Public transport
Shuttle bus out of Kamikochi, 25 minutes. Then the ropeway. Check the wind forecast the night before, because it closes in strong wind.
Where you sleep
A ryokan (traditional inn) in Okuhida Onsen, in a room with its own open-air bath.

DAY 8 · FRIDAY 30 OCTOBER

Out of the mountains

The long journey back to Tokyo, with one special dinner waiting at the end of it.

Morning

Have one last hot spring bath before breakfast. Then drive to Matsumoto, through the mountains and down along the river, and return the car at the station.

Afternoon

Take the Azusa limited express back to Shinjuku, two hours and forty minutes. From there, cross the city to Ueno and check in to the hotel. Rest in the afternoon, because tomorrow starts with a very early alarm.

Evening

Dinner at Kagurazaka Sushi Yasaka, a small restaurant serving Edo-style sushi, the traditional Tokyo style. Expect to pay 10,000 to 15,000 yen per person, and it needs to be booked well in advance. Kagurazaka itself is a charming old neighbourhood of narrow stone lanes, and a lovely place for a last evening, just the two of you.

Driving
Okuhida to Matsumoto. About 60 km and 1h15. This is the last drive of the trip.
Public transport
Azusa limited express to Shinjuku, 2h40, then the metro across the city to Ueno.
Where you sleep
A hotel in Tokyo, in Ueno or Nippori, right next to the station for the airport train.

Book this week

You are seven weeks out, and this is peak season for the autumn colours, so the best places fill up fast. This is the order that matters, starting with the hardest to get.

  1. A hot spring lodge inside Kamikochi26 to 28 October. It is the last month before the valley closes for winter, and this is the hardest bed of the whole trip to find. Start here.
  2. Tsumago: tsumugi tei, Nagiso - booked24 to 26 October, 2 nights. Free cancellation until 24 September, in case a room comes up at an inn inside Tsumago itself.
  3. Okuhida, a room with a private open-air bath29 October. You only have one night here now, so it matters that you get the right type of room.
  4. Sushi in Kagurazaka30 October, evening. Book directly with the restaurant, or through a restaurant booking or concierge service.
  5. Her International Driving PermitFrom AA Singapore. You can walk in, it costs about S$20, and it is valid in Japan.
  6. Your JAF licence translationIt takes about two weeks by post. Start it now so that you can share the driving with her.

The two Tokyo hotels and the rental car can safely wait another week. Nothing in the list above can.

The driving licences

This is the one thing that could stop the trip at the rental car desk, so it is worth getting right early.

YOUR WIFE / SINGAPORE

She is the driver.

Singapore signed the 1949 Geneva road traffic convention, which is the one Japan recognises. That means a standard International Driving Permit from AA Singapore is fully valid in Japan. It costs about S$20, you can walk in without an appointment, and it is done in a day.

YOU / FRANCE

A French international permit is not accepted here.

France signed a different convention, so Japanese rental desks will refuse a French International Driving Permit. What you need instead is an official Japanese translation of your French licence from JAF, the Japan Automobile Federation. It costs about 4,000 yen and takes about two weeks by post.

Get her permit first: that alone makes the trip possible. Your translation is the backup that lets you take turns at the wheel.

Always carry your original licence together with the permit or the translation. The rental desk will check both, and so will the police if you are stopped.

Before you go

A few small things that make a real difference in the mountains at this time of year.

It will be cold

In late October, mornings in Kamikochi are between -3 and +3 degrees, with frost on the ground. During the day it warms up to about 12 degrees. Bring warm layers, gloves and a hat. This is mountain weather, not holiday-resort weather.

Shoes, not boots

Every walk on this trip is on a well-kept trail. A good pair of hiking shoes is all you need, and nothing on the itinerary requires crampons or spikes.

Get an ETC card

Ask for an ETC card with the rental car. It is a card that pays the motorway tolls automatically. The tolls add up quickly, and it saves you stopping to queue and pay at every toll gate.

Carry cash

Mountain inns, the Kamikochi shuttle bus and the small shops in the old post towns often only take cash, so keep a good amount of yen on you.

Ask for a kashikiri bath

In Kamikochi the hot spring baths are shared and separated into men's and women's. Ask each lodge for a 貸切風呂 (kashikiri-buro): a private bath you can book for the two of you, usually for about 45 minutes.

Check the ropeway

The Shinhotaka ropeway stops running in strong wind or snow. Check the forecast the night before you go and be ready to change the day's plan if needed.
